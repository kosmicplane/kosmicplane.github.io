CALTECH SURF · AMBER LAB · 2026

Safety & Contingency Certificates for Autonomous Landing

Two parallel contributions during the fellowship: a safety-critical landing research program using certificates and contingency, and hands-on work on ATMOS, a separate free-flying spacecraft testbed.

Research questionHow can a vehicle pursue a nominal landing target while preserving multiple feasible contingency alternatives in real time?
The presentation frames the system around MPC for nominal task behavior, CLFs for stability, CBF/HOCBF constraints for safety, Poisson fields for complex obstacle geometry, and connected funnel/ROA constructions for contingency-preserving motion.
Nominal MPC→Poisson safety representation→HOCBF / CLF-QP→funnel chain / ROAs→r-out-of-p contingency→simulation & hardware tests
01

Nominal predictive control

The nominal command is produced by a finite-horizon model predictive controller. It predicts the local vehicle motion, penalizes tracking error and control effort, enforces state/input limits, and applies only the first optimized action before replanning.

\[\min_{u_{0:N-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(x_k-x_k^{ref})^TQ(x_k-x_k^{ref})+(u_k-u_k^{ref})^TR(u_k-u_k^{ref})+(x_N-x_N^{ref})^TQ_f(x_N-x_N^{ref})\]
02

Poisson safety field

Obstacle geometry is converted into a smooth differentiable scalar field instead of constructing one analytical barrier per obstacle. Occupancy defines the free domain \(\Omega\), obstacles define \(\partial\Omega\), and the field gradient becomes the geometric input to the safety filter.

\[\Delta h(y)=f(y),\quad y\in\Omega,\qquad h(y)=0,\quad y\in\partial\Omega\]

Poisson ↔ funnel hardware comparison

The same physical obstacle environment was used to compare how the two safety representations shape the executed route. This clip is placed here because it is direct operating evidence of the method comparison, rather than a generic gallery item.

03

Barrier and Lyapunov certificates

For a velocity-level model, the CBF condition constrains the command to keep the state inside the safe set. For acceleration-level flight, HOCBF structure is used because obstacle position has relative degree two with respect to acceleration. CLF terms provide attraction toward the active landing objective while slack preserves feasibility.

\[\nabla h(p)^T u\ge -\alpha h(p)\]
\[\ddot h+\alpha_1\dot h+\alpha_0 h\ge 0\]
04

Connected ellipsoidal funnels

The route is represented by overlapping local regions rather than a single global tube. Each cell is a Lyapunov sublevel set; neighboring certified cells overlap so the controller can hand off between them. Growth stops at obstacles or workspace limits. This gives a connectivity-aware corridor and provides explicit local progress structure.

\[\mathcal E_i=\{x:(x-c_i)^TP_i(x-c_i)\le 1\},\qquad \mathcal E_i\cap\mathcal E_{i+1}\ne\varnothing\]

Online route reconstruction under obstacle changes

The funnel is not treated as a frozen route. As obstacle geometry changes, local cells are regrown/reconnected so the certified corridor can be rebuilt around the updated free space.

05

Contingency and r-out-of-p availability

Each candidate landing zone has a CLF and certified region of attraction. The combinatorial condition does not require every target to remain reachable; it preserves at least \(r\) of \(p\) alternatives while the nominal objective is pursued. A failed or invalid target can therefore be abandoned while the controller diverts to a surviving certified option.

\[h_j^{ROA}(x)=c_j-V_j(x),\qquad \tilde h_r(x)=\max^{(r)}\{h_1^{ROA},\ldots,h_p^{ROA}\}\]

Contingency growth and alternative landing routes

The route family is evaluated against multiple landing alternatives so that the nominal objective is pursued without exhausting all safe options. The visualization below shows the evolving corridor / alternative structure used in this stage of the work.

Measured implementation evidence

The benchmark suite separates the high-rate CLF-CBF-QP from slower planning and field-synthesis components. The native condensed MPC benchmark ran with a 6.76 ms median and 8.29 ms p95 over 1,000 solves; the native active-set CLF-CBF-QP had a 0.89 ms median and 1.11 ms p95. The 2-D Poisson pipeline, including domain construction, assembly, solve and derivatives, measured roughly 33–50 ms mean depending on solver. These timings characterize the tested implementation and hardware/software configuration; they are not universal complexity claims.

KAUST VSRP · NETLAB · 2026

SWARMSYM — High-Fidelity Swarm Network-as-a-Service Simulation

A research framework in which UAV motion, relay topology, radio feasibility, fault events and service recovery evolve inside the same experiment rather than being analyzed in isolation.

Research questionHow should a drone swarm be simulated when the swarm is itself a communication service and packet delivery is valid only if the embodied relay path remains physically and radio-feasible?

Why the framework is structured this way

The working manuscript treats SNaaS as three coupled systems: a robotic swarm, a communication service and an operator-controlled experiment. The central design decision is therefore to couple embodiment, networking and repeatable evidence generation inside one runtime loop. A robotics-only simulator can show convincing motion while missing outages; a network-only simulator can report feasible links that are impossible once geometry and UAV motion are imposed.

Mission configuration→ROS 2 coordinator→Isaac Sim↔PX4 / Pegasus↔Sionna RT / link service→packet gate→fault / recovery log

Time-varying relay graph

Each UAV is a node whose position changes over time. Candidate communication edges therefore depend on the current geometry and active-node set.

\[G(t)=(V(t),E(t)),\qquad d_{ij}(t)=\|p_i(t)-p_j(t)\|_2\]

Link budget and capacity

Sionna provides propagation-aware quantities. Received power and noise are converted to SNR; capacity is then used as one feasibility criterion for service execution.

\[\gamma_{ij}=P_{r,ij}-N_B,\qquad C_{ij}=B\log_2\!\left(1+10^{\gamma_{ij}/10}\right)\]

Feasibility gate

Packet progress is not a decorative animation: a hop advances only when the current link satisfies the configured geometric and radio thresholds.

\[\chi_{ij}=1\iff d_{ij}\le d_{max},\;\gamma_{ij}\ge\gamma_{min},\;C_{ij}\ge C_{min}\]

Path / branch availability

For a serial relay path, availability requires all constituent links to be feasible. Parallel and forest topologies expose branch-level and subtree redundancy instead of forcing one global chain.

\[\chi_P(t)=\prod_{(i,j)\in P}\chi_{ij}(t)\]
MODEL

Service path, branch state and failure-aware forwarding

For chain operation, an end-to-end relay path is feasible only when every active hop passes the same execution gate. In parallel/forest modes, each branch keeps its own packet state and outage reason; a failed relay invalidates incident links before topology recomputation.

\[\chi_{P}(t)=\prod_{(i,j)\in P}\chi_{ij}(t),\qquad \chi^{F}_{ij}(t)=\chi_{ij}(t)\,\mathbf{1}_{i otin F(t)}\,\mathbf{1}_{j otin F(t)}\]

The implementation also records a feasibility margin, so an outage can be attributed to range, SNR, capacity or failed-node constraints rather than being treated as an undifferentiated failure.

1Design a missionWorld, UAV count, topology, service endpoints, ranges and fault schedule.
2Execute embodied motionIsaac Sim with PX4/Pegasus-compatible vehicle control.
3Evaluate radio linksSionna-based path loss, SNR, capacity and delay.
4Gate servicePackets pause when a hop becomes infeasible.
5Inject faultsNode failure / healing and standby promotion change the active topology.
6Export evidenceEvent logs, branch state and communication metrics support replay and analysis.

Fault-aware recovery

When a relay fails, the runtime removes that node from the active service graph, recomputes connectivity using the surviving feasible links and can promote a standby UAV when the scenario permits. The manuscript formalizes this as a failure-monotone recomputation policy: failures reduce the available graph, while restoration is only claimed when a feasible survivor path exists.

Validation strategy

My work included framework integration, mission/topology workflows, live telemetry, communication-aware execution, fault injection and recovery experiments. Analogous simulations were also compared against AirPAW-derived data / scenarios to assess whether communication-performance trends were physically plausible. The quantitative validation campaign remains ongoing; the framework is therefore described here as a simulation research instrument rather than as a field-validated RF model.

A reproducible ROS 2 sensing foundation developed before higher-level VIO / SLAM: acquisition, timestamps, calibration, quaternion attitude estimation, network transport, visualization and embedded deployment.

IMU model

Raw angular rate and acceleration are treated as true signals plus slowly varying bias and measurement noise.

\[\omega_m=\omega+b_g+n_g,\qquad a_m=a+b_a+n_a\]

Quaternion attitude estimation

A Madgwick/Mahony-style AHRS propagates gyroscope motion and corrects drift using measured reference vectors.

\[\dot q=\tfrac12 q\otimes\omega-\beta\frac{\nabla f(q)}{\|\nabla f(q)\|}\]

Important scope distinction

The reports document the implemented inertial / depth platform and its readiness for later EKF/UKF, VIO and SLAM integration. Those later fusion layers should not be described as fully deployed outcomes unless a specific experiment demonstrates them. The work completed here is the calibrated, networked and reproducible sensing infrastructure on which those algorithms can be built.

UDEA · ALUNA · 2024–2025

CubeSat-Class Atmospheric Descent Probe

This is the Universidad de Antioquia / Volta atmospheric probe project — distinct from ATMOS at Caltech. The work combined descent dynamics, a directional multi-Pitot array, CFD, wind-tunnel calibration and post-flight reconstruction.

Research questionCan a small descent payload reconstruct airspeed, attitude and aerodynamic behavior from short, noisy flight data by combining physical models with IMU, GPS and directional pressure sensing?
CAD / sensor placement→wind-tunnel calibration→CFD interpretation→flight integration→recovery→trajectory reconstruction

Descent dynamics

Gravity and aerodynamic drag provide the physical backbone of the reconstruction.

\[D=\tfrac12\rho V^2C_DA\]

Pitot conversion

Calibrated dynamic pressure provides an independent airspeed estimate that complements inertial and GPS information.

\[q=\tfrac12\rho V^2,\qquad V=\sqrt{\frac{2q}{\rho}}\]

Directional array

Four Pitot ports were positioned to make the measured pressure pattern sensitive to flow direction, not merely speed.

CFD + experiment

External / internal flow simulations were used to interpret stagnation and wake behavior and compare expected sensor response with wind-tunnel measurements.
