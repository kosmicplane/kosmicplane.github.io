CALTECH · ATMOS · 2026

ATMOS — Autonomy Testbed for Multi-purpose Orbiting Systems

Free-flying spacecraft testbed work focused on simulation-to-hardware integration, motion tracking, instrumentation and physical validation.

Engineering purposeUse a free-flying spacecraft testbed to connect simulation, external motion tracking, onboard electronics and repeatable physical testing in one laboratory workflow.
1Digital modelROS 2 / Gazebo / PX4 interfaces provide a reproducible software-side environment before hardware runs.
2Ground truthOptiTrack motion capture supplies external pose measurements for test supervision and validation.
3Hardware bring-upElectronics, networking and instrumentation are checked at bench level before free-motion experiments.
4Closed experiment loopCommands, measured response and ground-truth motion are compared to diagnose integration and control behavior.

Why a testbed matters

Simulation alone cannot expose timing, interface, actuator, tracking and instrumentation effects. ATMOS is used as a physical integration layer where software assumptions can be confronted with measured behavior before orbital deployment.

My scope

Repository-level integration, ROS 2/Gazebo/PX4 simulation support, motion-capture interfacing, IoT instrumentation, hardware bring-up and experimental testing. This work is distinct from the UdeA ALUNA atmospheric probe.

Official ATMOS / DISCOWER documentation ↗Roque et al. ATMOS paper ↗
Platform reference.ATMOS was introduced by Pedro Roque et al. as an open-source, modular spacecraft-analog platform designed to connect software-in-the-loop simulation with physical testing of multi-agent space-autonomy concepts. See Towards Open-Source and Modular Space Systems with ATMOS, IEEE Transactions on Field Robotics (2025), DOI 10.1109/TFR.2025.3632772.
Validation logic. The evidence shown here is hardware/integration evidence: software commands, external tracking and physical response are exercised together. It is not presented as an orbital qualification campaign or as a separate spacecraft-design project.
KAUST · NETLAB · 2026

SWARMSYM — Swarm Network-as-a-Service Framework

A communication-aware digital twin where relay feasibility is an execution constraint rather than a post-processing annotation.

Research questionHow can a swarm provide communication service while vehicle motion, relay topology, radio feasibility and failures evolve in the same runtime loop?
MODEL

Relay-service execution model

The simulator treats the swarm as a time-varying directed graph. Physical motion changes inter-node distance; the communication layer converts that geometry into path loss, SNR, capacity and delay; the feasibility gate then decides whether a packet is allowed to advance. Failures modify the active set before topology recomputation, so a visual path is not automatically treated as a valid service path.

\[G(t)=(V(t),E(t)),\quad d_{ij}=\lVert p_i-p_j Vert_2,\quad C_{ij}=B\log_2(1+\gamma_{ij})\]

Time-varying relay graph

The swarm is represented as a relay graph whose vertices and candidate links change with vehicle state.

\[G(t)=(V(t),E(t))\]

Link capacity

Sionna-derived link metrics are used to evaluate whether a hop has enough radio quality to advance the service.

\[C_{ij}(t)=B\log_2\!\left(1+\gamma_{ij}(t)\right)\]

Feasibility gate

A packet/relay hop advances only if geometric range, SNR and capacity requirements are simultaneously satisfied.

\[\chi_{ij}=\mathbf{1}[d_{ij}\le d_{max}]\,\mathbf{1}[\gamma_{ij}\ge\gamma_{min}]\,\mathbf{1}[C_{ij}\ge C_{min}]\]

Failure-aware recovery

Failed relays are removed from the active graph, topology is recomputed, and service resumes only after a new feasible path passes the same gate used during nominal operation.

1Mission definitionWorld, fleet size, topology mode, radio assumptions, failures and service targets are fixed for a reproducible run.
2Embodied motionIsaac Sim with PX4/Pegasus materializes the multi-UAV scene and vehicle state.
3ROS 2 coordinationMission, topology, telemetry and recovery state are exchanged through a shared runtime.
4Telecom evaluationSionna supplies distance/path-loss/radio metrics used by the feasibility gate.
5Fault injectionRelay failures and topology disturbances are injected as controlled experimental variables.
6EvidenceSNR, capacity, outage, topology changes and recovery events are logged for post-run comparison.
Validation scope. The framework is a high-fidelity simulation instrument, not a claim of field-proven radio performance. Comparisons against AirPAW-derived data and analogous scenarios are used to check whether simulated communication trends remain physically plausible while preserving reproducibility.
Technical basis represented in the manuscript:ROS 2 for coordination; Isaac Sim for embodied simulation; PX4/Pegasus for autopilot-accurate vehicle execution; NVIDIA Sionna for communication-aware link metrics; relay-graph and outage/recovery metrics for service evaluation.

NCU · TEEP & IIPP · 2025–2026

Embedded Inertial & Depth-Sensing Platform

A ROS 2 sensing infrastructure in which embedded computation, attitude estimation and transport timing were developed and measured as one system.

Engineering objectiveBuild a reproducible embedded sensing and ROS 2 transport stack whose timing and orientation estimates are stable enough to support later multi-sensor fusion.
IMU / ESP32→Jetson ROS 2 bridge→AHRS quaternion estimate→Fast DDS / Wi-Fi→RViz / logging→RealSense expansion

Quaternion AHRS

Gyroscope integration supplies short-time propagation while accelerometer / magnetometer feedback constrains long-term drift, following the Madgwick/Mahony filter family documented in the report.

\[\dot q=\tfrac12 q\otimes\omega-\beta\frac{\nabla f(q)}{\|\nabla f(q)\|}\]

Timing as a sensing variable

For distributed estimation, transport jitter perturbs the effective measurement time. The network was therefore treated as part of the sensing pipeline, not as an unrelated IT layer.

ROS 2 / Fast DDS

Custom DDS transport settings and a dedicated wireless network were evaluated with ROS 2 timing tools to reduce latency and jitter between embedded publisher and ground workstation.

Fusion readiness

The completed result is the calibrated IMU/depth/network substrate. EKF/UKF, VIO and SLAM appear in the reports as next-stage integration targets, not as fully validated outcomes of this internship.

sub-10 msend-to-end delay in the dedicated setup reported in the first-stage work

UDEA · ALUNA · 2024–2025

ALUNA — CubeSat-Class Atmospheric Probe

Atmospheric descent sensing and reconstruction using directional pressure measurement, inertial/GPS data, CFD and wind-tunnel calibration.

Engineering questionCan a compact descent payload reconstruct air-relative motion and aerodynamic behavior by combining physical models with directional pressure, inertial and GPS measurements?
CAD / Pitot geometry→wind-tunnel calibration→CFD interpretation→flight integration→recovery→post-flight reconstruction

Descent force model

Drag provides the first-order link between air-relative speed, atmosphere and deceleration.

\[D=\tfrac12\rho V^2 C_D A\]

Pitot relation

Directional pressure ports provide dynamic-pressure observations that can be converted to airspeed after calibration.

\[q=\tfrac12\rho V^2,\qquad V=\sqrt{2q/\rho}\]

Why CFD and tunnel tests were both used

CFD was used to inspect stagnation, wake and sensor-placement effects; wind-tunnel data supplied physical calibration and a check on whether the modeled response was qualitatively consistent with the built geometry.

Flight reconstruction

Recovered IMU, GPS and Pitot measurements were interpreted together rather than treating any single sensor as ground truth. The objective was consistency between kinematics, pressure-derived airspeed and the aerodynamic model.

Validation logic. The project combined three evidence layers: numerical flow prediction, controlled wind-tunnel measurements and recovered flight data. Numerical agreement is presented only where the supplied project documentation supports it; the page does not claim a fully identified aerodynamic model from the short flight record.

AIAA DESIGN/BUILD/FLY · 2021–2022

JACAMARÁ — Propulsion & Electrical Systems

Fixed-wing competition aircraft developed for the 2022 medical-supply mission.

Design problemMatch propulsion, battery and aircraft electrical architecture to a timed medical-supply mission while preserving takeoff, climb and rapid ground-staging margin.

Propulsion matching

Propeller / motor / battery choices were compared through thrust-current-voltage testing and manufacturer / propeller performance data. The design trade was not maximum static thrust alone; it had to satisfy energy, climb and takeoff constraints.

Electrical budget

ESC, receiver, servos and avionics loads were budgeted against battery capacity and wiring limits, while quick-turn battery swaps were incorporated into the five-minute staging workflow.

\[E_{mission}\approx\int_0^T V(t)I(t)\,dt\]

Weight and balance

Payload changes altered mass distribution, so CG / balance checks were updated as syringe and vial configurations changed.

Validation

Ground test cards covered thrust, current and voltage. The integrated aircraft then passed technical inspection and flew at the Wichita fly-off. A numerical final rank is intentionally omitted here because the supplied portfolio and later CV versions contain inconsistent placements.

Evidence standard. This page reports the verified design tasks and successful fly-off participation. It does not choose between conflicting ranking numbers found in separate supplied documents.
UPB · AIRCRAFT DESIGN · 2025

SHOTS — Electrical & Agricultural Spray Subsystem

Conceptual system design for the AIAA Safe High Operational Tempo Sprayer flight-research problem.

Conceptual design objectiveSize an integrated 28 VDC electrical architecture and high-throughput spray system that satisfies the SHOTS mission envelope with explicit power, endurance, voltage-drop and application-rate margins.

Required spray flow

Application rate, ground speed and boom width determine the volumetric flow required from the spray system.

\[Q_{L/s}=\frac{a_{L/ha}\,b_m\,V_{g,m/s}}{10^4}\]

Hydraulic power

Pump power is obtained from pressure rise and flow after accounting for efficiency.

\[P_{hyd}=\frac{\Delta p\,Q}{\eta}\]

Electrical energy

Mission phases were resolved into essential/main-bus current so generator loading, battery-only endurance and cable voltage drop could be checked.

\[E=\int V(t)I(t)\,dt\]

Application quality

Nozzle pressure/flow data and droplet-size reasoning were used to target coarse/very-coarse spectra while maintaining boom uniformity and high field throughput.

WIND-TUNNEL PROJECT · 2024

High-Lift Wing Section with Actuated Surfaces

Experimental high-lift study based on repeatable control-surface deflections and aerodynamic force reduction.

Experimental objectiveCharacterize how flap, slat and spoiler configurations alter the lift response and stall behavior of an instrumented wing section.

Coefficient reduction

Measured aerodynamic force is normalized by dynamic pressure and reference area to compare configurations across angle of attack.

\[q_\infty=\tfrac12\rho V^2,\qquad C_L=\frac{L}{q_\infty S}\]

Experimental method

Arduino-driven surfaces provided repeatable deflection states; a multi-point force setup and repeatable angle-setting procedure supported CL–α comparisons.

Reported outcome

The portfolio documents lift/drag curves across configurations and a qualitative improvement in stall margin. Numerical coefficients are not reproduced because the underlying raw table was not supplied with the portfolio package.

Numerical aerodynamic analysis shown only to the level supported by the available project evidence.

Numerical objectiveUse ANSYS external-flow CFD to inspect pressure / velocity structure around an airfoil at elevated speed and practice disciplined domain, mesh and aerodynamic-result interpretation.

Governing basis

What is claimed
